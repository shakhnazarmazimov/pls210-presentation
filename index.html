Trilingual Education & Academic Language Choice

PLS210 Research Proposal Presentation #1

Instructor: Dina Pisareva | Fall 2026

1. Research Question

"How do university students’ experiences with trilingual education shape their decisions to use Kazakh in academic communication in Kazakhstan in 2026?"

Empirical & Scoped Focus

  • Target Population: University undergraduate students in Kazakhstan.
  • Context & Time: Academic settings in 2026, following years of trilingual language policy implementation (Kazakh, Russian, English).
  • Core Phenomenon: Student agency, subjective experiences, and language choices in formal academic communication (essays, presentations, discussions).

2. Epistemological Positioning

Stance: Interpretivism

Justification

Language selection in an academic environment is not merely a mechanical or statistically predictable output of policy exposure. It is a social practice shaped by how individuals construct meaning, navigate identity, and perceive status within institutional contexts.

  • Why Interpretivism fits: Rather than establishing a quantitative cause-and-effect link (e.g., measuring hours of instruction vs. frequency of language use), this study examines how students interpret institutional language dynamics and how those interpretations inform their choices.
  • Interpretivist Logic: Social reality is constructed through subjective experiences. Understanding language choices requires investigating the meanings students assign to Kazakh, Russian, and English in academic spaces.

3. Social Science Contribution

The Empirical Puzzle

Kazakhstan’s state policy actively promotes trilingualism, positioning Kazakh as the primary state language alongside Russian for interethnic communication and English for global integration. However, in higher education, official policy expectations often diverge from actual daily academic choices made by students.

Contribution to Literature

  • Moves beyond top-down educational policy evaluations to focus on bottom-up student agency and lived experiences.
  • Provides qualitative insights into how official language policy is negotiated on the ground in academic environments in 2026.

4. Literature Review

The study engages three primary streams of political and sociological literature:

A. Top-Down Trilingual Policy vs. Implementation Practice

Existing research highlights the structural objectives of Kazakhstan's language policy but often treats students as passive recipients of state initiatives rather than active decision-makers.

B. Linguistic Capital & Symbolic Power (Pierre Bourdieu)

Language operates as a form of social capital. Students evaluate language choice based on perceived institutional returns, such as professor evaluations, academic prestige, and peer perception.

C. Language Ideologies & Academic Identity

Scholarship shows that academic language registers differ significantly from conversational fluency. Language anxiety, resource availability, and educational background heavily influence whether students feel confident using Kazakh for formal academic tasks.

5. Analytical Framework & Sensitising Concepts

In line with an interpretivist design, this study uses sensitising concepts rather than rigid hypothesis testing:

Sensitising Concepts

  • Perceived Academic Capital: How students judge the functional and symbolic value of Kazakh versus Russian or English in university settings.
  • Linguistic Confidence & Anxiety: The subjective feeling of mastery over academic terminology in Kazakh compared to conversational usage.
  • Institutional Language Norms: Implicit expectations from professors, peers, and department cultures.
